About

A chapter built for the parts of the path nobody formalizes.

Who we are, what we are for, and how the chapter is run.

Who we are

The American Physician Scientists Association is a national, student-run organization for trainees pursuing careers that combine medicine and research. It exists because that career is unusually hard to navigate from the inside: the training is long, the funding is its own discipline, and the people who have done it are scattered across departments.

We are the UC Irvine local chapter. Local chapters are the grassroots layer of APSA — the part that actually runs events on a specific campus, for the specific students there. Our job is to find where the gaps are at UCI and fill them.

Our mission

The mission of the local chapter is to mentor, support, and advocate for doctorate of medicine students who are physician-scientist aspirants at UC Irvine, with programming that is beneficial but not yet widely available or formalized. Article II, Chapter Constitution

Specifically, the chapter exists so that its members can:

  • Develop an understanding of medical research, including study design, scientific rigor, and the range of basic, translational, clinical, public health and computational approaches.
  • Meet, network with, and build collaborative relationships with physician-scientists.
  • Give and receive peer-to-peer and near-peer mentorship.
  • Better understand the challenges, pitfalls and opportunities in the training and career of a physician-scientist.
  • Advocate for the needs of physician-scientists to local, regional, state and federal government.
  • Adjust to the needs of our own student body, as those change.

One commitment runs underneath all of it: single-degree and dual-degree students get equal consideration. An M.D. student with a serious research interest belongs here just as much as an M.D./Ph.D. candidate.

How the work gets done

Seven committees

Every event the chapter runs belongs to a committee, and every committee answers to a specific part of the mission. Members can join any of them.

Mentorship

Fosters near-peer mentorship and professional relationships across every stage of training. As the chapter grows, this may extend to undergraduates considering the physician-scientist path.

Research

Creates opportunities to present work, receive feedback from peers and faculty mentors, and deepen understanding of methodology and scientific rigor. Owns the chapter's annual research symposium.

Technology

Builds practical, skeptical understanding of large language models and other AI tools in research, education and clinical practice — where they help, and where their limits demand caution.

Professional Development

Runs speaker series, workshops and educational programming with physician-scientists and other experts, and develops resources that strengthen members' professional skills.

Career Planning

Organizes advising sessions and panels on training pathways, specialty selection, research careers, funding, and the obstacles encountered throughout physician-scientist training.

Advocacy

Represents trainees at institutional, local, state and federal levels, including initiatives such as State Capitol Day. Positions taken in the chapter's name must be evidence-based and approved by a two-thirds vote.

Fundraising

Secures the resources that keep programming running, cultivating partnerships with the School of Medicine and other institutional and community stakeholders.

How the chapter is governed

The chapter is run by an executive committee of six officers: a President, Vice President, Secretary, Treasurer, Webmaster and School of Medicine Liaison. The President also serves as UC Irvine's Institutional Representative to national APSA, carrying information in both directions between our campus and the national organization.

The School of Medicine Liaison is deliberately reserved for a student who is not in the MSTP or M.D./Ph.D. program. It is a structural guarantee that single-degree students have a voice on the executive committee rather than an assurance that they are welcome.

Officers serve one-year terms and are elected each spring by anonymous ballot of the active membership. Any active member in good standing may be nominated, including by themselves; where three or more candidates stand for one office, voting is by ranked choice. A faculty advisor — a full-time UCI faculty member and established physician-scientist — provides continuity across those annual handovers.
