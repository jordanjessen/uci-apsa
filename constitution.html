Preamble

We, the members of the American Physician Scientists Association (APSA) do hereby establish this constitution in order that our purpose be realized to its fullest extent.

Article I – Name

This organization shall be known as the University of California, Irvine American Physician Scientists Association Local Chapter and will hereinafter be referred to as the local chapter.

Article II – Purpose

The local chapter understands and is committed to fulfilling its responsibilities of abiding by the University of California, Irvine policies. In extension of the purpose of the National APSA Chapter, the purpose of the local chapter is to mentor, support, and advocate for doctorate of medicine students (i.e., M.D., M.D./Ph.D., M.D./M.S., M.D./M.Eng., M.D./M.P.H., etc.) who are physician-scientist aspirants at UC Irvine with programming that is beneficial but not yet widely available or formalized.

Specifically, the local chapter will allow its members to:

  • Develop an understanding of medical research, including study design, scientific rigor, and the range of basic, translational, clinical, public health, and computational approaches.
  • Meet, network with, and build collaborative relationships with physician-scientists.
  • Provide peer-peer and near-peer mentorship to physician-scientist aspirants.
  • Better understand the challenges, pitfalls, and opportunities in the training and career of a physician-scientist.
  • Advocate for the needs of physician-scientists to the local, regional, state, and federal government.
  • Adjust to the needs of our local student body.

Article III – Membership

Membership in the local chapter is open only to students enrolled at the University of California, Irvine who are pursuing a medical doctorate (M.D.) degree, whether through a single-degree M.D. program or a dual-degree program (i.e., M.D./Ph.D., M.D./M.S., M.D./M.Eng., M.D./M.P.H., etc.). To become and to remain a member, a student must be in good standing with the University of California, Irvine School of Medicine, both academically and with respect to their behavioral and conduct record. Alumni may participate in chapter activities as invited guests but shall not vote or hold office. The local chapter distinguishes between two categories of membership: active members and inactive members.

Active Members

They must satisfy all requirements of general membership and, in addition, participate in at least 60% of the events held by the chapter each academic year. Membership shall be evaluated on an academic-year basis beginning July 1 and ending June 30. The Executive Committee may grant exceptions to attendance requirements for students on clinical rotations, significant research commitments, approved leave, or other reasonable extenuating circumstances. Active members have the right to:

  • vote
  • run for office
  • attend meetings
  • participate in chapter programming
  • serve on committees

Inactive Members

Members listed on the chapter’s membership roster who receive chapter communications but do not meet the participation requirement for active membership. Inactive members must maintain good academic and behavioral standing with the University of California, Irvine School of Medicine. Members who become inactive may reclaim active status by satisfying the participation requirements during the current academic year.

Requirements for Membership in the National APSA Chapter

Members seeking membership in the National APSA Chapter must likewise be in good standing with the University of California, Irvine School of Medicine, both academically and with respect to their conduct.

Membership Dues

As the local chapter is a student interest group within the University of California, Irvine School of Medicine, no membership dues shall be required of its members.

Membership enrollment, maintenance, and termination

Students become members by completing the chapter’s membership registration process and providing any information required by the chapter. A member is in good standing if they remain enrolled in an eligible M.D.-granting program at the University of California, Irvine School of Medicine and are not subject to academic dismissal, suspension, or disciplinary sanctions that would restrict participation in student organizations under university policy. Members on an approved leave of absence from the School of Medicine may retain membership status but shall not be eligible to vote or hold office until they return to active enrollment unless otherwise approved by the Executive Committee. Membership terminates upon graduation, withdrawal, or separation from the University of California, Irvine School of Medicine.

Requirements to Participate in Officer Elections

Candidates for elected office must be active members in good standing at the time of nomination and throughout their term of office.

Voting rights

Only active members may vote on constitutional amendments and officer elections.

Non-discrimination statement

The University of California, Irvine APSA Chapter openly admits students to its membership and does not discriminate on the basis of race, color, creed, sex, sexual orientation, marital status, personal appearance, age, national origin, political affiliation, physical or mental disability, or based on the rights secured by the First Amendment of the United States Constitution.

Article IV – Officers and Faculty Advisor

The APSA chapter will be governed as follows:

Officers of the local chapter must be active chapter members in good academic standing in their program of study at the University of California, Irvine School of Medicine. They are expected to demonstrate behaviors consistent with the highest standards and principles of the University of California, Irvine School of Medicine Student Code of Conduct, and to be recognized by their peers for superior reasoning, strong work ethic, and sound ethical judgment, as well as a curriculum vitae with evidence of scientific accomplishments such as peer-reviewed publications, presentations, and abstracts. The officers of the local chapter that constitute the executive committee shall be President, Vice President, Secretary, Treasurer, Webmaster, and the School of Medicine Liaison. Executive Committee members will be charged with the assigned duties described herein:

President/Institutional Representative (hereby referred to as President)

The President is charged with the overall operation of the APSA Chapter. They must ensure the continuance of programs that benefit the student body. Furthermore, the president must communicate frequently with the members and the national APSA organization. The president must promote the interests of the APSA Chapter within the university. An elected President (or other chapter officer) will preside at all chapter meetings. The President will serve as the Institutional Representative (IR) for the University of California, Irvine at the National APSA level or work closely with this individual, if an IR is already in place. The President may appoint committee chairpersons, shall present motions before the assembled body, and shall attend 90% of chapter meetings. The president shall also make every effort to attend the Annual Meeting of the American Physician Scientists Association or appoint a person such as the Institutional Representative to attend in their place.

Vice President

The Vice President is charged with ensuring the smooth election and transition of the officers after election, helping to provide continuity to the chapter. They are charged with maintaining a current e-mail list of chapter membership, and with serving as the Acting President when the President deems necessary or is unable to. Furthermore, in the event the President resigns or is removed from office, the Vice-President will assume the responsibility of President until the officers and advisory members can meet to vote for a new President.

Secretary

They will take minutes of all chapter meetings. These shall include a timeline of activities and programs, and any notes or suggestions submitted by the officers or chapter members in attendance. The Secretary shall also oversee the committees in charge of organizing individual events. These committees shall be comprised of one chairperson and at least one active general member who will plan and execute APSA-sponsored events. The Secretary will maintain contact with the committee chairs to ensure that adequate progress is being made such that the event will be ready by its planned date.

Treasurer

This person will record and maintain the records of expenditures for the APSA Chapter, including submission of forms necessary to request funds. They will attend Associated Medical Student Government (ASMG) meetings related to student group funding or appoint another officer to attend in their place.

Webmaster

They will be responsible for maintaining the local chapter webpage.

School of Medicine (SOM) Liaison

The SOM Representative is a position established to ensure stable and substantial communication between the APSA Chapter and the SOM. This position will be filled by an APSA Chapter member (see article III) actively enrolled in the SOM, but not the MSTP or MD/PhD program. The representative will be charged with the duty of ensuring that APSA maintains a strong relationship with the SOM and is a visible organization for all aspiring physician-scientists at the University of California, Irvine. The SOM Liaison will be elected from the Chapter’s membership according to Article VI and is a member of the executive committee. This person is charged with promoting the mission of the APSA Chapter within the SOM by advertising events, recruiting additional members, and spreading information about the chapter. Electing two APSA members to the SOM Liaison positions is allowable, so long as one is the in the MS1-2 (pre-clinical) years of training and the others is in the MS3-4 (clinical) years.

Faculty Advisor

The faculty advisor must be a full-time member of the University of California, Irvine faculty, selected by the membership of the APSA Chapter. The Faculty Advisor is responsible for generating ideas, serving as a resource person, and providing continuity to the organization. To complement the stated goals of the APSA Chapter, the Faculty Advisor will be an established physician-scientist who has significant experience in mentoring aspiring physician-scientists.

Article V – Committees

As a chapter, APSA will sponsor regular events that are specifically developed to meet the training needs of aspiring physician-scientists (See article II). The following committees will be chaired by an officer (See article IV) and will include, but not be limited to, the following:

Mentorship

This committee will be led by the Mentorship Chair and will act to foster a collaborative culture of near-peer mentorship and professional relationship building among aspiring physician-scientists. Emphasis will be placed on connecting members across all stages of training through peer-to-peer and physician-scientist mentorship, facilitating the exchange of knowledge, guidance, and professional experiences. As the chapter grows, the committee may expand its efforts to include mentorship opportunities for undergraduate students interested in physician-scientist careers.

Research

This committee will be led by the Research Chair and will act to promote research engagement and scientific collaboration among members. It will organize opportunities for members to present their research, receive constructive feedback from peers and faculty mentors, and deepen their understanding of research methodology, scientific rigor, and the spectrum of basic, translational, clinical, and computational research. The committee will also oversee the planning and development of the chapter’s annual research symposium and other scholarly programming.

Technology

This committee will be led by the Technology Chair and will act to promote understanding of and responsible engagement with large language models (LLMs), other forms of artificial intelligence (AI), and other relevant technologies in the context of the physician-scientist career. Emphasis will be placed on helping members understand how these tools work, where they are useful, and where their limitations require caution, so that members can apply them thoughtfully and with appropriate scrutiny. The Technology Chair will be responsible for acquiring and creating accurate, timely, and relevant information regarding LLMs and other relevant technologies and their evolving role across research, education, and clinical practice. The committee will also organize accurate, timely, and relevant events, including workshops and demonstrations, that increase members’ understanding of how to use LLMs and other relevant technologies effectively and responsibly throughout the physician-scientist training pathway and career.

Professional Development

This committee will be led by the Professional Development Chair and will act to provide members with opportunities for professional growth. Emphasis will be placed on organizing speaker series, workshops, and educational programming featuring physician-scientists and other experts, as well as developing educational resources that strengthen members’ professional skills and understanding of the physician-scientist training pathway.

Career Planning

This committee will be led by the Career Planning Chair and will act to provide members with opportunities to explore, plan, and discuss the diverse career pathways available to physician-scientists. It will organize career advising sessions, panel discussions, and educational programming that address training pathways, specialty selection, research careers, funding opportunities, and the challenges and opportunities encountered throughout physician-scientist training.

Advocacy

This committee will be led by the Advocacy Chair and will act to represent and advance the interests of physician-scientist trainees at the institutional, local, state, and federal levels. Emphasis will be placed on educating members about science and health policy, organizing participation in advocacy initiatives such as State Capitol Day, and engaging with elected officials and institutional leadership to advocate for policies that support physician-scientist training and biomedical research. Advocacy undertaken in the Chapter’s name shall bear a direct relationship to the training, funding, or professional environment of physician-scientists, and shall be grounded in peer-reviewed evidence or the published positions of the national APSA, AAMC, or comparable professional bodies; where evidence is contested or the membership is divided, the Chapter shall favor education over the adoption of a position. Any public position or statement advanced in the Chapter’s name shall require approval by a two-thirds vote of the Executive Committee. The Chapter shall not endorse or oppose any candidate for public office or political party, and shall comply with applicable law and the policies of the University of California, Irvine. Chapter advocacy shall not imply that the Chapter speaks for or is endorsed by the University of California, Irvine, AMSG, or national APSA.

Fundraising

This committee will be led by the Fundraising Chair and will act to secure financial resources that support the chapter’s mission and long-term sustainability. It will coordinate fundraising initiatives, cultivate partnerships with the School of Medicine and other institutional and community stakeholders, and identify additional funding opportunities to expand programming, support member activities, and increase access to educational and professional development opportunities.

Article VI – Chapter Operations

1. Voting Eligibility

Only active members, as defined in Article III, are eligible to vote in chapter elections, constitutional amendments, and any other matter requiring a vote of the membership. Members must be in good academic and behavioral standing with the University of California, Irvine School of Medicine at the time of voting.

Unless otherwise specified in this Constitution, all motions shall be decided by a simple majority of active members present, provided that a quorum has been established.

A quorum shall consist of one-third (1/3) of all active members or ten (10) active members, whichever is fewer.

2. Election Process

Officer elections shall be held annually during the Spring academic quarter. Newly elected officers shall assume responsibility following completion of the chapter’s succession and transition process.

The Executive Committee shall announce the election timeline to the membership in advance of nominations and voting.

Candidates may be nominated by any active member of the chapter, including self-nomination. All nominees must:

  1. Be active members in good standing.
  2. Meet the officer eligibility requirements described in Article IV.
  3. Consent to nomination and responsibilities of the office.
  4. Submit a brief statement of interest describing their qualifications, goals, and vision for the office.

Candidates may run for multiple offices but may hold only one elected executive office.

Current officers may seek re-election to the same office or election to a different office provided they remain eligible under Article IV. Voting shall be conducted by anonymous ballot. The Secretary shall administer the election unless they are a candidate for another officer position, in which case the President shall appoint another officer who is not seeking election to oversee the voting process for that particular office. Election results shall be verified by at least two executive officers who are not candidates for the office being counted and shall be announced to the membership following certification of the results.

The candidate receiving the simple majority of votes shall be elected. To avoid the need for runoff elections, voting will be done by ranked-choice for offices with three or more candidates. If no eligible candidate is nominated for an office, nominations shall remain open until an eligible candidate is identified. Should a vacancy remain following elections, the Executive Committee may appoint an eligible active member to serve until the next regularly scheduled election.

3. Term Limits

Executive officers shall serve one-year terms. Officers may be re-elected provided they continue to satisfy all eligibility requirements described in Articles III and IV. No member may simultaneously hold more than one elected executive office. Should an officer resign or otherwise become unable to carry out their responsibilities before completing a term, the Executive Committee shall fill the vacancy in accordance with this Constitution.

4. Removal

An officer may be removed from office for failure to fulfill assigned duties, violation of this Constitution, repeated unexcused absence from meetings, misconduct inconsistent with the standards of the University of California, Irvine School of Medicine, or actions that substantially interfere with the mission of the chapter. Any officer of the APSA Chapter in violation of the chapter’s purpose or Constitution may be removed from office by the following process:

  1. Submission of a written request signed by at least three active members.
  2. Written notification to the officer describing the concerns and requesting that the officer attend the next scheduled meeting to address the membership.
  3. Discussion by the Executive Committee and membership.
  4. A two-thirds (2/3) majority vote of active members present, provided quorum has been established.

If the President resigns or is removed from office, the Vice President shall assume the duties of President until a successor has been selected in accordance with this Constitution.

Vacancies in any other executive office shall be filled by appointment of the Executive Committee until the next regularly scheduled election.

5. Meetings

The chapter shall hold regular general membership meetings throughout the academic year as determined by the Executive Committee.

Notice of each meeting, including the date, time, location, and agenda, shall be provided to members in advance whenever practicable.

General meetings may include the following:

  1. Call to order and attendance.
  2. Approval of the previous meeting minutes.
  3. President’s announcements and meeting agenda.
  4. Guest speaker or research presentation, when applicable.
  5. Officer reports.
  6. Committee reports.
  7. Old business.
  8. New business.
  9. Discussion and voting on motions.
  10. Upcoming events and announcements.
  11. Open forum for member comments.
  12. Adjournment.

Meetings may be conducted in person, virtually, or in a hybrid format.

Unless otherwise specified in this Constitution, motions shall pass by a simple majority vote of active members present.

6. Officer Meetings

The Executive Committee shall meet regularly throughout the academic year and additionally as necessary to conduct chapter business.

Officer meetings shall generally precede membership meetings and shall include, but not be limited to, discussion of:

  1. The agenda for the upcoming membership meeting.
  2. Officer responsibilities and progress toward assigned duties described in Article IV.
  3. Committee activities and updates described in Article V.
  4. Budget status, funding requests, and financial planning.
  5. Planning of upcoming events and educational programming.
  6. Ongoing initiatives, operational issues, and strategic priorities.
  7. Assignment of responsibilities and action items.

The President shall prepare the meeting agenda, and the Secretary shall record and maintain meeting minutes.

Executive Committee decisions shall be made by majority vote of officers present. A majority of executive officers shall constitute a quorum.

7. Succession and Transition Action Items

To ensure continuity of leadership and sustained chapter operations, the Executive Committee shall conduct a formal succession and transition process each academic year. All transition activities shall be completed before the end of the academic year to provide adequate time for training, mentorship, and onboarding prior to the conclusion of the academic year.

The transition process shall include, but not be limited to, the following action items:

  1. Elect the incoming Executive Committee in accordance with the procedures described in this Constitution.
  2. Conduct joint meetings between outgoing and incoming officers to review the responsibilities, expectations, annual timeline, and strategic objectives associated with each executive position.
  3. Review the chapter Constitution and operating procedures and identify any proposed amendments or revisions for consideration by the membership.
  4. Transfer, if needed, all chapter records and institutional knowledge, including meeting minutes, membership rosters, committee documents, planning materials, event templates, budgets, financial records, fundraising documentation, and historical archives.
  5. Transfer, if needed, ownership and administrative access to all chapter resources, including email accounts, cloud storage, websites, communication platforms, social media accounts, calendars, and other organizational assets.
  6. Review the status of all ongoing initiatives, committee projects, planned events, funding requests, grant applications, partnerships, and other outstanding responsibilities to ensure continuity between administrations.
  7. Introduce incoming committee chairs and officers to committee members, faculty advisors, institutional representatives, National APSA leadership, and other collaborators necessary for the continued operation of the chapter.
  8. Review the chapter’s financial status, including available funds, anticipated expenditures, fundraising efforts, and pending reimbursement or funding requests.
  9. Develop or update the chapter’s projected calendar of meetings, educational programming, research events, advocacy initiatives, networking opportunities, fundraising activities, and other major chapter functions for the upcoming academic year.
  10. Provide incoming officers with guidance regarding best practices, lessons learned, recurring challenges, and recommendations to promote effective leadership and organizational continuity.
  11. Complete any additional transition activities deemed necessary by the Executive Committee to ensure the continued success, stability, and long-term growth of the chapter.

Following completion of the transition process, outgoing officers shall remain available to provide reasonable consultation and mentorship to their successors throughout the remainder of the academic year to facilitate a smooth transfer of responsibilities.

Article VII – Finances

The APSA Chapter will finance the activities it engages in by the following means:

  1. Secure funding and support from the University of California, Irvine School of Medicine through the Associated Medical Student Government (AMSG).
  2. Utilizing available funds, if any, from partner organizations such as the MSTP, Institute for Clinical and Translational Science (ICTS), and School of Medicine Dean’s Office at the University of California, Irvine.
  3. Fundraising within the University of California, Irvine School of Medicine.
  4. Applying for grants through the national APSA chapter.

Article VIII – Amendments

The constitution is binding to all members of the APSA Chapter. But the constitution is not binding unto itself. Any member in good standing may submit proposed constitutional amendments to the executive committee. The executive committee will disseminate the proposed amendment in writing to the members at least 30 days prior to voting at the next scheduled chapter meeting. A two-thirds membership majority is required for amendment approval.

Following elections of new officers, the outgoing and incoming officers will review the constitution. Any proposed changes or amendments will be presented to the membership at a general meeting and will be voted on. A two-thirds membership majority is required for approval.

Article IX – Status within the University of California, Irvine School of Medicine

The local chapter should be recognized as a student interest group in the University of California, Irvine School of Medicine. This local chapter’s primary goal is supporting medical doctorate students, making equal consideration of the interests of both single-degree (i.e., M.D.) and dual-degree (e.g., M.D./Ph.D., M.D./M.S., M.D./M.P.H., etc.) students. The local chapter’s secondary goal is supporting, mentoring, and advocating for undergraduate students at the University of California, Irvine. Members of the local chapter are only those who are single- and dual-degree M.D. students at the University of California, Irvine School of Medicine.
